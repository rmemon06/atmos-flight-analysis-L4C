My analysis · 1 of 5

Evening out the laser beam

01 · What the camera saw

The laser is off-centre. A particle only changes the light by a few %, so at the top it's invisible.

02 · Shrink 16×, median filter

A 240 px median keeps the typical value, so rings and fibres vanish. Shrinking first makes it fast.

03 · Blur, enlarge: the beam

The laser's shape on its own: how bright it is at every pixel, with no particles.

04 · Camera ÷ beam

Every pixel becomes a fraction of normal brightness. Rings show up everywhere.

Why divide, not subtract · a particle blocking 5%

Subtracting leaves it 50× fainter at the top. Dividing gives the same signal anywhere.

My analysis · 2 of 5

Finding the particles

01 · The fringes

Each particle makes rings, dark then bright. Hunting for dark spots would split one particle into many.

02 · Subtract 1, square

−0.16 and +0.14 both square to about 0.02, so dark and bright both count.

03 · Blur: one hill each

A 20 px blur melts each ring pattern into one hill. Every hilltop over 2× noise is a candidate.

04 · Spin 180°: the centre

Spin the rings 180° and lay them on themselves. Off-centre they smear; at the true centre they line up.

05 · Is it round?

Average the patch in circles to get a perfectly round version, then compare. 1.0 = identical. 0.6 or more is a particle.

My analysis · 3 of 5

Size and distance from the rings

01 · Average in circles

Average the brightness around each thin circle. The rings become one wiggly line; a neighbour's rings average out.

02 · Ring spacing: distance

Against radius² the rings are evenly spaced. Wider spacing = further away. First guess: 4.4 mm.

03 · Fade-out: size

Same distance, from the formula: a bigger particle's rings fade sooner. Where they fade gives the size.

04 · Fit the exact formula

Adjust size and distance together until the formula matches the measured line: 23 µm across, 4.7 mm away.

05 · Can I trust it?

I made fake holograms of particles with a known size and distance, 5–80 µm and 2–20 mm away, and ran the same code on them.

My analysis · 4 of 5

"Non-round" detections were
two particles

  1. I noticed

    Some detections weren't round, so they were being thrown out as "other".

  2. The cause

    Two similar rings side by side look the same rotated 180° about their midpoint, so my centre-finder settled between them.

  3. The fix

    When a detection isn't round, search around it for round centres and keep those instead.

My analysis · 5 of 5

Faint particles were
being ignored

  1. Mask

    The usable-area mask was too cautious. I cut it from 60 to 4 units of light, and tested that pure noise still gives no false detections.

  2. Energy

    Faint rings don't stand out from the background by energy alone. I loosened the threshold from 6 to 2 and let shape decide.

  3. Noise

    Noise made faint rings look less round. Roundness now takes out the expected noise before judging.
