My analysis · 1 of 4

Evening out the laser beam

01 · What the camera saw

The laser is off-centre. A particle only changes the light by a few %, so at the top it's invisible.

02 · Shrink 16×, median filter

A 240 px median keeps the typical value, so rings and fibres vanish. Shrinking first makes it fast.

03 · Blur, enlarge: the beam

The laser's shape on its own: how bright it is at every pixel, with no particles.

04 · Camera ÷ beam

Every pixel becomes a fraction of normal brightness. Rings show up everywhere.

Why divide, not subtract · a particle blocking 5%

Subtracting leaves it 50× fainter at the top. Dividing gives the same signal anywhere.

My analysis · 2 of 4

Finding the particles

01 · The fringes

Each particle makes rings. Faint ones are weaker than the noise, so you can't pick them out ring by ring.

02 · Try every depth

Refocus to depths from 2.5 to 26 mm. Most dots come into focus 4.4 mm away: that's where the particles are.

03 · Refocus: rings → dots

Running the light backwards gathers every ring into one dark dot. Faint rings add up, so faint particles appear.

04 · Mark the dark dots

Every spot 8× darker than the noise around it counts. Each particle is kept once, at its sharpest depth.

05 · Is it a particle?

In focus, a particle is a compact dot. A fibre or a streak is a line, so anything stretched more than 4× is left out, and so are big fibres that black out the area around them.

My analysis · 3 of 4

Size and distance from the rings

01 · Average in circles

Average the brightness around each thin circle. The rings become one wiggly line; a neighbour's rings average out.

02 · Ring spacing: distance

Against radius² the rings are evenly spaced. Wider spacing = further away. First guess: 4.4 mm.

03 · Fade-out: size

Same distance, from the formula: a bigger particle's rings fade sooner. Where they fade gives the size.

04 · Fit the exact formula

Adjust size and distance together until the formula matches the measured line: 23 µm across, 4.7 mm away.

05 · Can I trust it?

I made fake holograms of particles with a known size and distance, 5–80 µm and 2–20 mm away, and ran the same code on them.

My analysis · 4 of 4

Faint particles were
being missed

  1. I noticed

    In one busy patch I could count about 25 ring patterns by eye. My detector found 7.

  2. The cause

    It squared the rings and blurred them into hills. That throws away dark vs bright, so faint rings drowned in the noise.

  3. The fix

    Refocus instead: running the light back gathers every ring into one dot, so faint rings add up. On fake holograms: 57 of 60 found, up from 44.

My analysis · the key code

A lens, written in code

src/optics.py
class Refocuser:    def __init__(self, light):        # 1 Split the light into flat waves, once per frame        self.spectrum = fft2(np.sqrt(light))        H, W = light.shape        fx = fftfreq(W, PIXEL)[None, :]   # across        fy = fftfreq(H, PIXEL)[:, None]   # down        # 2 How far each wave slips out of step, per metre        a = 1 - (WAVELENGTH * fx)**2 - (WAVELENGTH * fy)**2        self.delay = 2*np.pi/WAVELENGTH * (1 - np.sqrt(a))    def brightness(self, z):        # 3 Send every wave back z, add them up again        field = ifft2(self.spectrum * np.exp(1j * z * self.delay))        return np.abs(field)**2   # what a camera z away would see

Simplified. The real version also pads the edges, so rings don't wrap round, and uses 32-bit numbers for speed.

  1. 1

    Split the light into flat waves

    One FFT turns the frame into millions of flat waves, each crossing at its own angle.

  2. 2

    Work out each wave's slip

    A tilted wave falls out of step with a straight one as it travels. This is the exact physics of light, not an approximation.

  3. 3

    Send them back, add them up

    Undo the slip for depth z with one inverse FFT. The rings fold back into dots.

0.8 sper depth, for a 16-million-point FFT
34depths per frame. The first FFT is done once and reused.

Looking back

What I learned

01 · Physics

Let the physics drive the code

Dividing by the beam, not subtracting, gave the same signal everywhere. Refocusing, which is just optics, beat my image-processing trick: 583 particles, not 216.

02 · Testing

Never trust a detector without ground truth

It looked fine until I counted a patch by eye: 7 found, about 25 there. Now fake holograms with known answers test the code: 26 tests.

03 · Embedded

Real-time C++ from year one

Our first logger read temperature, pressure, CO₂ and airflow over I²C, and wrote every reading to the SD card as it flew.

04 · Leadership

Leading a team takes a plan

Our first year was chaotic. As team lead I planned the second year properly, and we built and flew a far harder payload.

Limits now, fixes next

  1. NowNext

  2. Stuck particles inflate the count

    Track particles frame to frame

    Count only the ones that move.

  3. The laser was off-centre and cut out

    The top got about 50× less light, and it went off at frame 97 of 900.

    Centre the beam and keep it on

    Not only from their rings.

    34 depths, each a full refocus of 12 MP.

    Run the depths in parallel on a GPU

    Each depth is independent of the others.
